      <h3>Violence as a Condition: Structure, Composition, and the Use of Lethal Force</h3>
      <div class="meta"><span class="m-auth">with Matthew Kyle Ellis</span><span class="m-ven">Journal of Conflict Resolution</span></div><div class="awards"><span class="aw"><i class="fas fa-landmark" aria-hidden="true"></i> Requested by the U.S. Congressional Research Service for distribution</span></div><div class="detail"><div class="ab"><span class="lbl">Abstract</span>Since 2014, Russia's Wagner Group has expanded from driving Russian foreign policy across Africa and the Middle East into an integral part of the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. We first draw on the one-sided violence (OSV) literature to show how organizational incentives explain Wagner's violence against civilians pre-November 2021. Despite the static context of those incentives, Wagner's OSV lethality increased dramatically after the invasion. Introducing pre-combat experience (PCE) as an explanatory factor, we provide novel theoretical logic and empirical evidence for how individual-level experience aggregates to unit-level violence.<div class="links"><a href="https://www.skytheacademic.com/files/violence_as_a_condition.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PDF</a><a href="https://dataverse.harvard.edu/citation?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/2FB6KD" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Replication archive</a></div></div></div>

      <h3>Who Keeps the Peace? Gendered Effects in UN Peacekeeping</h3>
      <div class="meta"><span class="m-ven">International Studies Quarterly</span></div><div class="awards"><span class="aw"><i class="fas fa-award" aria-hidden="true"></i> ISA Midwest Lynne Rienner Award</span><span class="aw"><i class="fas fa-award" aria-hidden="true"></i> ISA IO Section Finkelstein Prize (Special Mention)</span></div><div class="detail"><div class="ab"><span class="lbl">Abstract</span>Why are some peacekeepers more effective than others? Most analyses of peacekeeper effectiveness fail to account for gendered effects, while work on gender in peacekeeping hasn't taken advantage of recent advances in data. By unifying the literature on female peacekeepers and trust, this paper provides the first comprehensive framework explaining how women enhance UN peacekeeper effectiveness. Using two-way fixed-effects models and optimal matching, I provide the first quantitative empirical evidence that including more women in a force can dramatically increase its effectiveness against rebel one-sided violence.<div class="links"><a href="https://www.skytheacademic.com/files/who_keeps_the_peace.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Current draft</a><a href="https://github.com/skytheacademic/who_keeps_the_peace" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Replication archive</a></div></div></div>

      <h3>Double Delegation: The UN Private Security Contractor Database</h3>
      <div class="detail"><div class="ab"><span class="lbl">Abstract</span>Principal-agent scholars rarely go past the second level on the delegation chain, especially for delegation from international organizations to private security contractors (PSCs). I argue that delegating authority a second time to PSCs operating outside the direct control of IOs like the UN harms UN authority derived from local actors while increasing state delegation costs. I build the concept of double delegation and assemble a descriptive database of PSC use in UN missions.<div class="links"><a href="https://www.skytheacademic.com/files/double_delegation.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Current draft</a></div></div></div>
